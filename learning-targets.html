    Section 5 What Are Learning Targets, and How Do I Earn Them?

    Instead of one high-stakes test per topic, this course uses mastery-based grading built around 16 Learning Targets (LTs): 10 are marked CORE (non-negotiable prerequisites for College Algebra), 5 are elective content targets, and 1 (LT16) is earned through attendance rather than content. Every LT is graded proficient (1) or not-yet (0) — no partial credit, no partial credit, did we mention no partial credit?
    🔗
    • (LT 1) Explain and justify what it means for a number or object to be a solution to an equation or inequality or their systems.
      🔗
      🔗
    • (LT 2, CORE) Formulate, solve, and interpret linear equations, and evaluate solutions in context.
      🔗
      🔗
    • (LT 3) Determine, represent, and interpret equations of lines, and analyze how slopes and intercepts describe relationships.
      🔗
      🔗
    • (LT 4, CORE) Analyze and solve absolute value equations, represent their solutions, and justify reasoning.
      🔗
      🔗
    • (LT 5, CORE) Model, construct, and solve systems of linear equations from word problems, and evaluate the reasonableness of solutions.
      🔗
      🔗
    • (LT 6) Solve, represent, and interpret linear inequalities in one and two variables, and compare different forms of solution sets.
      🔗
      🔗
    • (LT 7, CORE) Apply and evaluate the properties of exponents to simplify expressions, and classify and construct polynomials by degree and number of terms.
      🔗
      🔗
    • (LT 8) Perform, verify, and connect operations on polynomials to broader algebraic structures.
      🔗
      🔗
    • (LT 9, CORE) Select, apply, and justify appropriate factoring techniques for polynomials.
      🔗
      🔗
    • (LT 10, CORE) Construct and solve equations by factoring, and evaluate solutions for accuracy and context.
      🔗
      🔗
    • (LT 11) Perform, simplify, and analyze operations on rational expressions.
      🔗
      🔗
    • (LT 12, CORE) Construct, solve, and interpret rational equations, and evaluate potential restrictions on solutions.
      🔗
      🔗
    • (LT 13, CORE) Perform, simplify, and justify operations on radical expressions.
      🔗
      🔗
    • (LT 14, CORE) Construct and solve radical equations, and evaluate solutions to identify extraneous roots.
      🔗
      🔗
    • (LT 15, CORE) Perform and justify operations on complex numbers and analyze and solve quadratic equations with complex solutions.
      🔗
      🔗
    • (LT 16) Marked absent six or fewer times throughout the semester (see Section 8).
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    The retake system is generous, so use it. Each LT is assessed on regular quizzes (about 20 over the semester, 6-20 minutes each), and you get 3 attempts to reach proficiency on each one. Once you hit proficient, you’re done — no need to retake for style points. You can retake up to 4 quizzes per week, so if Tuesday’s quiz on linear inequalities doesn’t go your way, there’s room for a comeback later that week. The exception: LT14 and LT15 don’t get the normal retake treatment — your one remaining shot at those two is the final exam (see Section 6).
    🔗
    All work must be shown on every assessment to earn credit. Grading rewards correct, clear, logical presentation with proper units, notation, and labeled functions — a right answer with no work, or a mess of one, does not earn credit. If I can’t follow your reasoning, neither can you, six months from now.
